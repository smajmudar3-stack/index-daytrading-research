## The number that is the hurdle

> **Under zero-edge calibration, P(10×) = 1.47% — roughly 1 in 68.**

Any claimed strategy, guru or track record must beat **1.47%**, not beat zero.

Full zero-edge distribution (σ_log = 2.494, calibrated to the anchors from
report 01):

| multiple | P | per million |
|---|---:|---:|
| ≥2× | 6.26% | 62,646 |

| **≥10×** | **1.47%** | **14,695** |
| ≥23× | 0.60% | 6,000 ✓ *(matches report 01)* |
| ≥100× | 0.096% | 963 |

## The finding that overturns the obvious reading

**The "97% lose" base rates are NOT evidence against this plan.** This is the
most important analytical point in the report and it cuts against its own
Priority-1 section.

Chague's 97%-lose, ESMA's 74–89%, and Barber's 80% are **fully consistent with
1.5% of the same population hitting 10×.** High variance produces both tails
simultaneously — the *same* σ = 2.494 that yields P(10×) = 1.47% also yields
P(down 90%+) = **63%** and a median outcome of **4.4% of starting capital**.

> **The loss statistics *are* the price of the tail, not an argument against
> reaching for it.**

What the literature actually establishes is narrower and more useful:

1. **Retail has negative edge, not zero edge.** Bryzgalova: 12.6% average quoted
   spread on retail-favoured weeklies, **28.7% OTM**, 23.5% on trades ≤$250. That
   friction shifts μ below the zero-edge case, so **real-world P(10×) is below
   1.47%**, not at it.
2. **Persistence destroys you.** Every study finding catastrophe conditions on
   **repetition** — Chague requires 300 trading days, Barber requires trading in
   consecutive years. **A single-shot attempt is not the population these papers
   measure.**
3. **The one place skill demonstrably exists is unavailable.**

> **Repetition, not variance, is what the literature actually condemns.**

That independently reproduces report 12's bold-play conclusion from an entirely
different direction — empirical rather than theoretical.

## Does the winner rate exceed luck?

### Taiwan — yes, by ~1.7×, and it is small

Barber, Lee, Liu & Odean (*JFM* 2014) build exactly this test — a 2×2 of
winner/loser in year *y* vs *y+1*:

| sample | expected by chance | **observed** | ratio |
|---|---:|---:|---:|
| all day traders (n = 3.8M) | 3.9% | **6.6%** | **1.69×** |
| >NT$600k (n = 2.3M) | 3.1% | **6.2%** | **2.00×** |

Both reject the luck null at **p < 0.01**. **Skill is real and measurable.**

And it is small: 2.7pp of excess on a ~20% base. The skilled cohort is **<1% of
the population**, identifiable only *ex post* after a full prior year of profits,
and its edge expresses as **37.9 bps/day net** — extraordinary as an edge, but a
**compounding wage, not a lottery ticket.** It does not transfer to a
single-shot 10×.

Also: *"more than 75% of all day traders quit within two years"*; survival at
1/2/3 years = 44% / 24% / 15%. And **no learning** — unprofitable veterans return
next year with 95.3% probability vs 96.4% for profitable ones.

### Trading championships — no. The winners are fully explained by luck.

**World Cup Trading Championships** — real brokerage accounts, "subject to final
audit," $10,000 futures minimum. The only genuinely verified retail competition.

| year | winner |
|---|---|
| **1987 — Larry Williams** | **11,376% (~114×)** |
| 2025 futures | 324.7% |
| 2025 forex | 600.9% |
| 2026 futures | 498.2% |

> Zero edge alone predicts **1.5 ≥10× finishers per 100 entrants.** The
> championship fields hundreds of leveraged accounts per year. Modern winners top
> out at **3×–7×**.
>
> **The best-of-field is running at or below the zero-edge benchmark.**

Larry Williams' 114× is roughly a 1-in-1,000 draw, and across ~40 years and
thousands of cumulative entrants, **one such observation is exactly what luck
predicts.**

**There is no options division** — futures and forex only. So the best-documented
body of verified retail multiples contains essentially **no options data**.

### Brazil — nobody 10×'d, and there was no right tail at all

Chague, De-Losso & Giovannetti, verified verbatim: *"97% of them lost money, only
**0.4% earned more than a bank teller (US$54 per day)**, and the top individual
earned only **US$310 per day with great risk (a standard deviation of
US$2,560)**."*

**⚠️ Correction to our own brief:** the "1.1% earned above minimum wage" figure
we had been carrying is **not in the paper**. It says **0.4% above a bank-teller
wage**. Use 0.4%/$54.

The single best performer out of every Brazilian who persisted 300+ days ran a
daily Sharpe of ~0.12 — indistinguishable from a lucky gambler. **No fat right
tail existed.**

## The 10× distribution does not exist in the literature

Searched across four formulations. **No published study reports the fraction of
retail options traders achieving a 10×, or any terminal-multiple distribution.**

This is structural, not a search failure:
1. Every paper measures **return rates or dollar P&L**, not terminal wealth
   multiples on a fixed stake.
2. They **condition on survival** (300 days; consecutive years), which
   mechanically truncates exactly the tail in question — *the person who turns
   $5k into $50k in six weeks and stops is invisible in all of them.*
3. Account-level terminal-multiple data sits with brokers, and none has released
   it.

## Cornwall Capital — the honest split between structure and setup

**Verified:** 2003–2012 average annual compounded **net 40% (52% gross)**; the
subprime short *"generated 80 times the initial investment."*
**Not verified:** the $110k → $120M figure and the LEAP mechanics — those are from
*The Big Short*, not Wikipedia.

> The **repeatable** part is the method: buy convexity where the market prices a
> binary outcome as continuous, and size so total loss is survivable. The
> **non-repeatable** part is the setup. Cornwall's own long-run number is the
> tell — **40% CAGR sustained, with the 80× confined to one trade in one
> dislocation.** They did not have a machine that produces 80×; they had one
> correct call on a generational mispricing, embedded in a career of 40%.

## Blow-ups — unverified, but the structural point survives

Karen Bruton/Hope Advisors (SEC 2016), James Cordier/OptionSellers.com (Nov 2018,
clients in **debit** to the clearing broker), LJM (Feb 2018, −80% in two days).
**None confirmed at source** — SEC.gov rate-limited every attempt.

The point that survives regardless: **all three were short-premium.** Smooth
equity curve, then the whole curve paid back at once. That is the **opposite**
profile from a long-convexity attempt.

> Their relevance is not "big winners lose it back." It is that **the strategies
> which feel safest to a retail account are the ones with the debit-balance
> tail.** A long-premium attempt cannot lose more than $5k. That is a genuine
> structural advantage of the stated plan.
