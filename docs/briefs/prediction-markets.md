# Prediction markets as an automated profit source for a US retail account, September 2026

## 1. Mechanics of the Polymarket 5-minute BTC/ETH/SOL markets

**Resolution.** Since 7 August 2026 00:00 UTC every 5-minute, 15-minute and 4-hour up/down market settles on a Chainlink TWAP Data Stream: a 30-second averaging window for 5-minute markets, 60 seconds for 15-minute and 4-hour. Open and close both come from the same feed ([Genfinity](https://genfinity.io/2026/08/12/chainlink-twap-data-streams-polymarket-crypto-markets/), [CoinDesk 2026-08-07](https://www.coindesk.com/business/2026/08/07/how-a-five-second-trick-let-traders-drain-millions-from-polymarket)). Before that it was a single Chainlink snapshot at expiry, which is what the manipulation study below exploited. The feed is available over Polymarket's public WebSocket "Real-Time Data Service" without credentials.

**Fees.** Formula is `fee = shares × feeRate × p × (1−p)`, taker-only, charged at match. The January 7, 2026 schedule for 15-minute (then 5-minute) crypto markets peaked at about 1.56% of notional at p=0.50, which corresponds to feeRate 0.0625 (0.0625 × 0.25 = 1.5625%). That number is reported by a bot operator's own log ([dev.to, Feb 2026](https://dev.to/lkto1m/february-2026-changed-polymarket-forever-heres-what-happened-to-my-bots-numbers-2fi5)) and by [Finance Magnates](https://www.financemagnates.com/cryptocurrency/polymarket-introduces-dynamic-fees-to-curb-latency-arbitrage-in-short-term-crypto-markets/) as "~3.15% of a 50-cent contract", the same figure expressed per share price. By March 30, 2026 the crypto feeRate was 0.07, i.e. $1.75 per 100 shares at 50c (1.75% of notional), the highest category; politics/finance 0.04, sports 0.05, geopolitics 0 ([Polymarket docs](https://docs.polymarket.com/polymarket-learn/trading/fees), [Crypticorn](https://www.crypticorn.com/polymarket-fees-explained/)). Makers pay nothing; a maker rebate pool returns 20% of collected taker fees daily in USDC (docs); the Dune analysis puts it at 20–25%. Polymarket US has its own schedule: taker θ=0.0695 ($1.74/100 at 50c), maker rebate θ=−0.0125 (−$0.31/100), and volume-tiered taker rebates of 10/25/50% above $250k/$1M/$10M monthly ([docs.polymarket.us](https://docs.polymarket.us/fees)).

**Microstructure.** Selected crypto up/down markets hold every taker order for 250 ms and re-validate before matching ([order lifecycle docs](https://docs.polymarket.com/concepts/order-lifecycle)). The earlier 500 ms delay was removed in February 2026 (dev.to above). Tick sizes are 0.01/0.001/0.0001 per market; no documented minimum order size. Book depth on 5-minute BTC is roughly $5k–15k per side ([CoinDesk 2026-02-21](https://www.coindesk.com/markets/2026/02/21/how-ai-is-helping-retail-traders-exploit-prediction-market-glitches-to-make-easy-money)); a Dune dashboard shows the largest single bet since Feb 12 at $28.7k and the biggest 5-minute window at $403k ([dune.com/kingnana](https://dune.com/kingnana/polymarket-analytics)). Quoted spreads on 15-minute BTC are 1–2c calm, 5–10c in volatility (Crypticorn).

**Who trades.** Dune's address classification (Sep 2025–Mar 2026): 6,189 bot addresses made 56 million trades in 5-minute markets against 166,000 trades from 43,898 casual addresses; bots are 55–62% of volume at $6–7 per trade; Polymarket collected $23.7M net fees in the 83 days after Jan 7 ([blockchain.news summary of the Dune blog](https://blockchain.news/news/polymarket-fast-markets-volume-bots-fees-analysis); the original [dune.com/blog post](https://dune.com/blog/polymarket-fast-markets) now 404s).

## 3. Pricing efficiency and calibration

- **Kalshi** (46,282 contracts, 313,972 priced positions, 2021–Apr 2025): prices are good forecasts and improve into close, but contracts under 10c lose over 60% of stake, contracts over 50c earn a small positive pre-fee return, and the average return on all contracts is about **−20%**. Makers out-earn takers; both show the bias ([Whelan, "Makers and Takers"](https://www.karlwhelan.com/Papers/Kalshi.pdf)).
- **Polymarket** (588M trades, 614,883 markets, Nov 2022–Mar 2026): favourites +0.28% to +0.83% terminal return depending on weighting, longshots −6% to −19%; strongest in Crypto and Politics, absent in Sports; **fees and rebates excluded** ([arXiv 2609.12878](https://arxiv.org/html/2609.12878)). A sub-1% gross edge on favourites does not survive a 0.07 feeRate unless you are the maker.
- **Short-horizon crypto is well calibrated**: across 353M trades on both venues, crypto at 0–6 hours has calibration slopes near 1.0; the compression that creates favourite-longshot edge grows with horizon, from 0.99 at 0–1h to 1.32 beyond a month ([arXiv 2602.19520](https://arxiv.org/html/2602.19520v2)). So there is no documented statistical mispricing to harvest in a 5-minute market; whatever edge exists there is speed or manipulation.
- **Executable arbitrage** on Polymarket: YES-side payoff violations last a median 16 seconds, NO-side 8 seconds; the 2024–25 total was ~$1.12M, 97% needing the on-chain converter; 76.9% of combinatorial opportunities are executable for an average of **14.8 shares** ([arXiv 2608.00666](https://arxiv.org/html/2608.00666v1), [arXiv 2605.00864 NBA](https://arxiv.org/html/2605.00864v1)).
- **Oracle-latency arb** is documented by practitioners only: Binance and the Chainlink aggregate diverge 0.3–0.8% for 10–30 seconds in fast tape (dev.to). The response was the 250 ms taker hold, a fee that peaks exactly where the arb lives, and TWAP settlement.

## 4. Polymarket vs Kalshi gaps, fees, and US access

- **Gap size.** No measured study exists. Practitioner pages claim "consistent 2–5% spreads" and a 3c example (YES 0.45 on one venue, NO 0.52 on the other), with windows lasting seconds and a 1,000-contract order eroding the 3c gap to 1.6c on slippage ([trevorlasn.com](https://www.trevorlasn.com/blog/how-prediction-market-polymarket-kalshi-arbitrage-works), [PredTerminal](https://predterminal.com/blog/kalshi-vs-polymarket-live-arbitrage-august-2026-whales-price-gaps-safe-trading)). The academic view is that cross-venue divergence is structural (different resolution sources, deadlines, definitions) and only closes by holding both legs to settlement, which is capital-heavy ([arXiv 2601.01706](https://arxiv.org/html/2601.01706v1), [OddsShopper on rule mismatches](https://www.oddsshopper.com/articles/prediction-markets/kalshi-vs-polymarket-settlement-rules)).
- **Kalshi fees.** Taker `ceil(0.07 × C × P × (1−P))`, max 1.75c per contract at 50c; makers pay ~1.75% × P(1−P) (~0.44c at 50c) since April 2025 ([MarketMath](https://marketmath.io/platforms/kalshi), Whelan). A Kalshi bot builder measured 1.55c average taker fee on KXBTC15M fills against a 0.67c gross edge from "buy the leading side" over 6,298 windows, and stopped on 2 August 2026 ([botforkalshi](https://www.botforkalshi.com/blog/kalshi-bitcoin-bot)). Kalshi 15-minute BTC settles on a 60-second average of one-second CF Benchmarks observations, a different index than Polymarket's Chainlink TWAP, so the "same" 15-minute market can resolve differently.
- **US access.** Global polymarket.com is **close-only for US IPs**; Polymarket US launched 2 Dec 2025 under a CFTC-designated exchange, dropped its waitlist in May 2026, is blocked in roughly eight states, and Polymarket asked the CFTC in April to lift the ban on the main exchange with no decision yet ([CoinDesk 2026-04-28](https://www.coindesk.com/policy/2026/04/28/polymarket-seeks-cftc-approval-to-reopen-main-exchange-to-u-s-traders), [startpolymarket](https://startpolymarket.com/countries/united-states/)). VPN use breaches ToS §2.1.4 and Polymarket now blocks VPN IP ranges and freezes accounts ([Prediction News](https://predictionnews.com/story/polymarket-cracking-down-on-vpn-users-evading-geolocation-blocks-3bda2e71)). I could not confirm whether Polymarket US lists the 5-minute crypto markets or exposes a trading API; treat that as unknown.

## 6. Risks

- **Oracle disputes.** UMA: $750 bond, 2-hour challenge window, 24h commit + 24h reveal, so a disputed market takes at least 50 hours; 1,150+ disputes in the first five months of 2026; a WSJ investigation found the ten largest wallets cast over half the votes in most disputes and one in five had a voter with a stake in the outcome; the March 2025 minerals-deal vote cost NO holders ~$7M with no refund ([OddsShopper](https://www.oddsshopper.com/articles/prediction-markets/uma-oracle-polymarket-disputes), [crypto.news](https://crypto.news/how-prediction-markets-resolve-uma-optimistic-oracle/)). Crypto up/down markets bypass UMA, so this hits sports/politics legs, exactly the cross-venue trades.
- **Settlement manipulation** works against you as a maker: the XRP case cost the bots "a year of profits overnight".
- **Liquidity at size:** $5–15k a side, $0.08 median per conversion, 14.8-share executable baskets.
- **Platform changes without notice:** taker delay removed, fees added, feed switched, all within seven months.
- **Account and legal:** VPN detection, frozen funds, state bans; the $3.1M June 2026 frontend supply-chain hack ([CoinDesk](https://www.coindesk.com/markets/2026/06/27/polymarket-hack-updated-to-usd3-1-million-days-after-the-platform-promised-users-full-refunds)).

$5,000 → $50,000 in 5 months needs 9.85x, i.e. 58% a month compounded. 20%/year on $5,000 is $1,000.

Blunt summary: no strategy in this space has a public, verified track record that would compound $5,000 to $50,000, and the two with the best receipts (settlement manipulation, converter arbitrage) are respectively illegal and already at $0.08 a trade for outsiders.
