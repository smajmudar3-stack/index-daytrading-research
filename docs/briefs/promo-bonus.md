### 2. Realistic total over 5 months

Adding the pieces that are actually reachable with $5,000 of capital and an ordinary paycheck:

- **Bank direct-deposit bonuses**: 2–4 accounts realistically opened and rotated through in 5
  months (each bank normally pays once per person, often gated to once every 1–2 years) →
  roughly **$1,500–$3,000**. This bucket doesn't consume the $5,000 — it only needs a paycheck
  temporarily redirected.
- **Credit card sign-up bonuses**: 3–5 cards before hitting velocity limits like 5/24, bonuses
  realized only if genuine monthly spending (not the $5,000 itself, which should never carry a
  balance against a bonus this size) covers each card's $3,000–$5,000 minimum-spend window →
  roughly **$1,500–$3,500**, contingent on having that much real spend to redirect.
- **Brokerage transfer/IRA match**: on a $5,000 transfer at 1–3%, **$50–$150** per program, and
  usually only one or two programs are practical to actually complete inside 5 months given
  multi-year vesting windows.
- **Crypto referrals**: **$50–$150**, immaterial.
- **Sportsbook/matched betting**: **$200–$600** if pursued across several books, minus the vig on
  each hedge and now minus the OBBBA 10% non-deductible slice on any net loss leg — riskier and
  more time-consuming per dollar than the bank/card buckets, and geofenced by state.
- **Idle-cash yield** on the $5,000 itself at a realistic HYSA rate (call it ~4%) for 5 months:
  **~$80–100** — the honest floor, not a bonus.

**Realistic 5-month range: roughly $3,300–$7,500 total**, i.e. the $5,000 becomes something like
**$8,300–$12,500** in the best realistic case — a 65–150% gain, not the 10x implied by
$5,000 → $50,000.

**Time cost**: on the order of **40–80 hours** — account applications, ChexSystems/credit
monitoring, direct-deposit redirect paperwork twice each way per bank, tracking each bonus's
clawback/vesting date, and doing the resulting tax return (every dollar above is ordinary income;
none of it gets capital-gains treatment).

### 3. Risks

- **Clawbacks**: nearly every deposit/transfer bonus in this inventory has a hold period (90 days
  to 5 years); pulling funds early forfeits or reverses the match, sometimes with an account-
  closure fee on top.
- **Credit impact**: each card application is a hard inquiry; opening several accounts in a short
  window lowers average account age and can trip issuer-specific velocity rules (5/24 and
  similar), which then locks out the highest-value bonuses for up to two years.
- **Account closures / bonus-abuse flags**: banks and brokers can and do shut down accounts that
  show no organic activity beyond the bonus-qualifying deposit, clawing the bonus back after the
  fact.
- **Tax drag**: everything above is ordinary income at the marginal rate (call it 22–32% federal
  plus state) — the "$3,300–$7,500" is pre-tax.
- **Gambling-specific**: OBBBA's move to a 90% loss deduction (from 100%) for tax year 2026 makes
  break-even matched betting a guaranteed small tax loss; multi-accounting across sportsbook apps
  also risks a ban that forfeits any bonus in flight.

### 4. Verdict against $5,000 → $50,000 in 5 months

**Not close.** The realistic, fully-legal ceiling this session could support with fetched or
well-established figures is roughly **$3,300–$7,500 of extractable value over 5 months** — most
of it from bank and credit-card signup bonuses that don't even require deploying the $5,000 as
capital, for 40–80 hours of account administration, taxed as ordinary income, with real clawback
and credit-file risk if pushed harder than that. Reaching $50,000 from this playbook would
require roughly 7–15x more bonus volume than a single person can realistically source and qualify
for in 5 months without organic income/spend to match it — it is not a capital or edge problem,
it is a "there are not enough distinct bonus programs a real household can complete in one income
year" problem.
